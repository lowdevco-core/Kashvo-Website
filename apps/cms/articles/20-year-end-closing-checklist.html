In India the financial year ends on 31 March. The last week of March and the first weeks of April decide how smooth your tax filings, audits and loan renewals will be for the rest of the year. This checklist keeps the closing calm.

Before 31 March

1. Clear pending entries

Enter every sale, purchase, expense, return and payment up to 31 March. Chase suppliers for missing bills, since purchases without bills can't support your input tax credit.

2. Follow up on receivables

Send statements to every credit customer and push hard for collections before year end. Review very old balances and decide, with your accountant, whether any should be written off as bad debts.

3. Confirm balances with major parties

Exchange ledger statements with your biggest customers and suppliers. Differences are much easier to sort out now than during an audit.

4. Plan the stock-take

Schedule a physical count for 31 March evening or 1 April morning. Prepare count sheets by shelf or section, and freeze stock movement during the count.

On 31 March and 1 April

5. Count and value closing stock

Count everything, compare with your system and record the differences. Closing stock is usually valued at the lower of cost and realisable value. Damaged or expired items should be written down. Remember: closing stock directly affects your profit, so accuracy here matters a lot.

6. Count cash and reconcile banks

Count the cash in hand and match it with the cash book. Reconcile every bank account with the 31 March statement, including charges and interest.

In the first weeks of April

7. Start new invoice series

GST invoice numbers must be unique within a financial year, so most businesses start a fresh series on 1 April (for example INV/26-27/0001). Set this up before the first bill of the year.

8. Check GST for the year

  • Compare annual sales in your books with what was reported in GSTR-1.
  • Compare the input tax credit claimed in GSTR-3B with your purchase register and GSTR-2B.
  • Note differences for amendment in the time allowed, and for your annual return.

9. Review TDS and TCS

Make sure tax deducted at source on rent, contractor or professional payments was deducted, paid and reported, and collect TDS certificates from customers who deducted tax from your payments.

10. Finalise accounts and lock the year

Once your accountant has finalised the trial balance, P&L and balance sheet, lock the year in your software so nobody can accidentally edit old bills. Closing balances carry forward as opening balances for the new year.

11. Back up everything

Download a full backup of the closed year and store it safely. GST law requires you to keep records for several years, typically 72 months from the due date of the annual return.
