For most shops, stock is the biggest thing the business owns. It's also the easiest place to lose money quietly: items that expire, items that never sell, items that walk out the door, and fast movers that run out just when customers want them. These eight habits keep inventory under control.

1. Let every bill move the stock

The single biggest improvement is to stop maintaining stock separately. When sales reduce stock and purchases increase it automatically, your system is right by default. Manual stock registers fall behind within a week.

2. Set reorder levels for your fast movers

For each item that sells steadily, decide the level at which you reorder. A simple rule: the quantity you sell in the time a supplier takes to deliver, plus a few days of safety stock. If you sell 10 packets a day and delivery takes 3 days, reorder at about 40 to 50.

3. Sort items into A, B and C

  • A items: the 10 to 20% of items that bring most of your sales value. Check them weekly.
  • B items: steady sellers. Check monthly.
  • C items: the long tail. Review every quarter and stop reordering the ones that don't move.

This tells you where attention pays off.

4. Count a little, often

A full stock-take once a year means a year of errors piling up. Instead, count a few shelves every week (a cycle count) and correct differences straight away. Over a month you cover the shop, and you catch problems while they are small.

5. Use the right units

Buying in boxes and selling in pieces? Buying in sacks and selling by the kilo? Set up the unit and conversion once (1 box = 12 pieces), so purchases and sales both update stock correctly. Wrong units are behind many "missing stock" mysteries.

6. Track expiry where it matters

For medicines, food and cosmetics, record batch and expiry dates and sell oldest first. A monthly report of items expiring in the next 60 days lets you discount them before they become a total loss.

7. Watch dead stock

Run a report of items with no sales in 90 days. That's cash sitting on a shelf. Return it to the supplier, bundle it, discount it, and stop buying it.

8. Label everything with a barcode

Barcodes make billing faster and stop the "typed the wrong item" errors that throw stock off. If your products don't come with barcodes, print your own labels. See barcode billing for faster checkout.
