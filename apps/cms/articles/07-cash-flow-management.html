Plenty of profitable businesses run short of cash. Sales are good, the margins are fine, and yet there isn't enough in the bank on the 5th to pay the supplier. The gap is cash flow: the timing of money coming in versus money going out. The good news is that managing it doesn't need an MBA, just a short routine done every week.

Profit and cash are not the same

When you sell ₹1 lakh of goods on 30 days' credit, your profit and loss shows the sale today, but the cash arrives next month. Meanwhile you paid your supplier, your staff and your rent. Growth makes this worse: more credit sales mean more cash tied up in receivables and stock. That's why fast-growing shops often feel poorer than slow ones.

The 20-minute weekly routine

Step 1: Know today's position

Write down three numbers: cash in hand, bank balance (all accounts), and UPI or wallet balances not yet settled. Your cash book should give you these directly. If it doesn't match what's actually in the drawer and the bank, find out why this week, not at year end.

Step 2: List what's coming in

From your receivables, list the invoices due in the next four weeks and who owes them. Be honest: mark the customers who always pay late as late.

Step 3: List what's going out

Supplier bills due, rent, salaries, EMIs, electricity, and the GST payment around the 20th. Don't forget quarterly or annual payments like insurance and licence renewals.

Step 4: Look four weeks ahead

Start with today's balance, add expected receipts, subtract expected payments, week by week. Any week that dips below a safe cushion is a problem you can see coming and fix early.

Week 2 is the danger week. Knowing it now gives you time to collect early, delay a purchase, or arrange credit.

Five levers that improve cash flow

  1. Collect faster. Send statements every week, not every month. Follow up the day a bill falls due. See managing customer credit.
  2. Set credit limits. Stop billing more on credit to customers who are already over their limit.
  3. Buy closer to need. Excess stock is cash sitting on a shelf. Reorder levels help you buy what sells.
  4. Use supplier terms fully, but don't miss due dates. Paying on the due day, not early and not late, keeps both cash and goodwill.
  5. Keep a cushion. Aim to hold at least two to four weeks of fixed costs in reserve.
