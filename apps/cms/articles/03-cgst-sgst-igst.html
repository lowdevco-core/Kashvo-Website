GST is one tax, but it appears on your bill under three different names. Which one you charge depends on a single question: is the sale within your state or to another state?

The short version

  • Same state (seller and place of supply in one state): charge CGST + SGST, half each.
  • Different states: charge IGST, the full rate.
  • Union territories without a legislature, such as Chandigarh or Ladakh: CGST + UTGST replaces CGST + SGST.

The total tax your customer pays is the same either way. What changes is which government gets it.

Example 1: a local sale

A shop in Kochi sells a mixer grinder worth ₹4,000 (before tax) to a customer in Kerala. The GST rate is 18%.

Taxable value₹4,000.00
CGST @ 9%₹360.00
SGST @ 9%₹360.00
Invoice total₹4,720.00

Example 2: an interstate sale

The same shop sells the same mixer to a registered dealer in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu.

Taxable value₹4,000.00
IGST @ 18%₹720.00
Invoice total₹4,720.00

Same total, different split.

What decides "same state"?

It's the place of supply, not where the buyer's head office is. For goods, it's usually where the goods are delivered. For a walk-in customer buying over the counter, it's your shop's state, so counter sales are almost always CGST + SGST. For services the rules vary by service type, so check them for anything unusual.

Prices that include GST

Many retailers print MRP or shelf prices that already include tax. To find the taxable value, divide by (1 + rate). An item sold at ₹118 inclusive of 18% GST has a taxable value of ₹100 and tax of ₹18, split ₹9 + ₹9 for a local sale. Billing software should handle tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive prices on the same bill without you doing the arithmetic.

Why it matters when you buy

When you purchase stock, the GST you pay becomes input tax credit you can set against the GST you collect. There is a fixed order for using credits. IGST credit is used first, against IGST and then CGST and SGST. CGST credit can't pay SGST, and SGST credit can't pay CGST. This is one reason accurate purchase entries matter as much as sales bills.

Common mistakes

  • Charging IGST on a local B2B sale because the buyer's GSTIN "looks" out of state, without checking the place of supply.
  • Typing a party's state wrongly once, so every bill to them is wrong.
  • Rounding CGST and SGST separately so they differ by a paisa.
