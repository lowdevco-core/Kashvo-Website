Giving credit, or udhaar, is how Indian businesses build loyal customers. The contractor who buys cement every week, the hotel that takes vegetables daily, the regular household that settles at month end: credit keeps them coming back. But unmanaged credit is also how good businesses run out of cash. Here's how to keep the loyalty without the losses.

1. Decide who gets credit, and how much

Not every customer should get the same terms. A simple approach:

  • New customers: cash or UPI only for the first few purchases.
  • Regular customers: a credit limit based on what they buy in a typical two weeks.
  • Trusted, long-term accounts: a higher limit and longer terms, reviewed every quarter.

Write the limit and the payment terms (for example, 15 or 30 days) against each party. Your billing software should warn you when a new bill would take a customer over their limit.

2. Record every payment against the right bill

When a customer pays ₹15,000 against three bills, record which bills it settles. Otherwise you end up with a total balance but no idea which invoices are old and overdue. Matching payments to invoices also makes your ageing accurate: how much is 0 to 30, 31 to 60 and over 60 days old.

3. Send statements, not reminders

A polite statement listing each bill, each payment and the balance is much easier to act on than "please clear your dues". Send it on WhatsApp or email every week or fortnight for active credit customers. Many customers pay simply because they can see the exact amount.

4. Follow up on a schedule

WhenAction
Bill dateShare the invoice with the due date
Due dateFriendly reminder with a statement
7 days overdueCall; agree a payment date
30 days overduePause further credit until cleared

The key is consistency. Customers learn which suppliers follow up and pay those first.

5. Make paying easy

Put your UPI QR and bank details on every invoice and statement. The fewer steps between "I should pay" and "paid", the faster the money comes.

6. Watch the ageing report every week

Total receivables can look fine while one customer's balance quietly grows older. The ageing report shows it. Anything over 60 days needs a personal conversation, not another message.

7. Handle bad debts honestly

Sometimes money doesn't come back. Write it off properly in your books rather than carrying it for years. It gives you a true picture of profit and stops you counting money you will never see.
