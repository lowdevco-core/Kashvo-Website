The profit and loss statement, or P&L, answers the most important question in business: did we make money? Many owners only see it once a year when the accountant files returns. Reading it every month is one of the best habits you can build.

The shape of a P&L

Every P&L works top to bottom: start with what you sold, subtract what it cost, and see what's left.

Line by line

Sales

Your sales excluding GST. GST you collect belongs to the government, so it isn't your income. Returns and discounts are deducted.

Cost of goods sold (COGS)

What the goods you sold cost you to buy or make. It's calculated as opening stock plus purchases minus closing stock. This is why accurate stock matters: if closing stock is wrong, profit is wrong.

Gross profit and gross margin

Sales minus COGS. Gross margin, gross profit as a percentage of sales, tells you how much you keep from every ₹100 sold before running costs. Watch it month to month. A falling margin means rising purchase prices, heavy discounting, or stock loss.

Operating expenses

The cost of running the business: rent, salaries, power, transport, packaging, software, bank charges. Group them so you can see which ones are growing.

Net profit

What's left after everything. Note that the owner's drawings don't appear here. They come out of profit, not as an expense. See separating business and personal money.

Five questions to ask every month

  1. Is gross margin steady, rising or falling? Why?
  2. Which expense grew the most compared with last month?
  3. Is net profit higher than what the family takes out?
  4. Does the profit show up as cash, or is it stuck in receivables and stock?
  5. How does this month compare with the same month last year?

P&L vs balance sheet vs cash book

  • P&L: performance over a period. Did we make money?
  • Balance sheet: position on a date. What do we own and owe?
  • Cash book: movement of actual money. Where did the cash go?

You need all three. A business can be profitable on the P&L and still short of cash. Read cash flow management for that side.
