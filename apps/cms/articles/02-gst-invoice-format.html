A GST tax invoice is more than a receipt. It is the document your customer uses to claim input tax credit, and the record the tax department relies on when it matches your returns against your buyers'. Getting the format right is what keeps those numbers lining up.

The rules for what an invoice must contain are set out in the CGST Rules. Here is what that means in plain language.

The fields every tax invoice needs

  • Your name, address and GSTIN. Exactly as on your registration.
  • An invoice number. Unique for the financial year, in a running series. It can use letters, numbers, hyphens and slashes, up to 16 characters. Many businesses use one series per branch, such as KOC/24-25/0142.
  • Date of issue.
  • Buyer's name and address. For a registered buyer, their GSTIN too.
  • Place of supply with the state name and code, for sales to another state. This decides whether IGST applies.
  • Description of goods or services.
  • HSN code for goods, SAC code for services. How many digits you must show depends on your turnover. Using the right code from the start avoids corrections later.
  • Quantity and unit for goods.
  • Taxable value after any discount.
  • Tax rate and tax amount split as CGST and SGST (or UTGST), or as IGST.
  • Total invoice value.
  • Whether tax is payable on reverse charge, where that applies.
  • Signature or digital signature of the supplier or an authorised person.

CGST + SGST or IGST?

If the supplier and the place of supply are in the same state, the tax is split equally between CGST and SGST. An 18% item becomes 9% CGST plus 9% SGST. If they are in different states, the full 18% is charged as IGST. Getting this wrong means the tax goes to the wrong government, and fixing it is slow. Billing software should decide it automatically from the buyer's state. We explain this in detail in CGST, SGST and IGST explained.

B2C sales at the counter

For walk-in customers who aren't registered, you don't need their name or GSTIN for ordinary sales. Large B2C sales to another state need more details, and your billing software should ask for them when the amount crosses the limit.

Other documents you will need

  • Bill of supply if you are under the composition scheme or selling exempt goods. It shows no tax. See composition scheme billing.
  • Credit note when goods come back or you reduce the price after invoicing.
  • Debit note when you increase the amount after invoicing.
  • Delivery challan when goods move without a sale, such as on approval or for job work.

Mistakes we see most often

  1. Gaps or repeats in invoice numbers from keeping two bill books, or from editing an old Excel file.
  2. Wrong customer GSTIN, which means the buyer can't claim credit and will chase you to fix it.
  3. IGST charged on a local sale, or the other way round.
  4. Rounding differences between the invoice and the GST return, from rounding per line in one place and on the total in the other.
  5. Missing HSN codes, which show up as errors in the HSN summary of GSTR-1.
