The GST composition scheme is meant to make life simpler for small businesses. Instead of charging GST on every sale and claiming input credit on purchases, a composition dealer pays a small fixed percentage of turnover. It's simpler, but it changes how you bill. Here is what you need to know.

Who can opt in?

The scheme is open to businesses below a turnover limit, which is higher for traders and manufacturers of goods and lower for service providers, with lower limits in some special category states. Some businesses can't use it at all, including those making interstate sales of goods, selling through e-commerce operators that collect tax at source, and makers of certain notified goods. The limits and exclusions are revised from time to time, so confirm the current ones before you apply.

What changes on your bills

  • No GST on the bill. A composition dealer can't collect GST from customers.
  • A bill of supply, not a tax invoice. The document is titled "Bill of Supply".
  • A required declaration. It must say the business is a composition taxable person and not eligible to collect tax on supplies. Many businesses also show it on their signboard.
  • No input tax credit. GST you pay on purchases becomes part of your cost. You can't claim it back.

What a bill of supply should contain

  • Your name, address and GSTIN
  • A unique serial number and the date
  • Customer details where needed
  • HSN code and description of goods or services
  • Value of the supply, after discounts
  • The composition declaration
  • Signature

Example

A small bakery under the scheme sells a cake for ₹600. The bill shows ₹600, with no CGST or SGST lines. The bakery pays its composition tax on turnover each quarter from its own pocket. It doesn't add it to the customer's bill.

Returns are lighter too

Composition dealers pay tax quarterly through a simple statement and file one annual return, instead of the monthly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B of regular taxpayers. Your billing software should still keep clean sales and purchase records, because you need the totals for these.

Is composition right for you?

Composition suits you if…Regular GST may suit you better if…
You sell mostly to consumers (B2C)You sell mostly to registered businesses that want input credit
Purchases carry little GSTYou pay a lot of GST on purchases and would lose that credit
You sell only within your stateYou sell to other states
You want minimum paperworkYou plan to grow past the limit soon

Switching later

If your turnover crosses the limit, or you start interstate sales, you must move to regular GST and start issuing tax invoices. Billing software that supports both document types lets you switch without changing systems.

In Kashvo you can mark each customer and supplier as regular, composition or unregistered, so the GST on your purchases and sales is recorded correctly whichever scheme your trading partners use.
