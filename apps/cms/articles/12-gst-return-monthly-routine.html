For most regular GST taxpayers, two returns come round every month: GSTR-1, which reports your sales, and GSTR-3B, a summary return where you pay the tax. Filing them is easy when your records are clean, and painful when they aren't. This routine keeps it on the easy side.

The two returns in one line each

  • GSTR-1: invoice-level details of your outward supplies. Your buyers' input credit depends on it.
  • GSTR-3B: totals of sales tax, eligible input tax credit and net tax payable, paid in cash or credit.

Small taxpayers can opt for the QRMP scheme, which allows quarterly returns with monthly tax payments. Check due dates on the GST portal, since they differ by scheme and sometimes by state.

Week 1 of the new month: close the old one

  1. Finish data entry. Every sale, purchase, return and expense for last month is entered.
  2. Check invoice numbering. No gaps, no duplicates, no bills dated in the wrong month.
  3. Review credit notes. Returns and discounts after billing should have proper credit notes.
  4. Verify B2B GSTINs. A typo means your buyer's credit won't show up, and they'll call you about it.

Before GSTR-1 is due: check your sales return

  • Compare total taxable value and tax in your sales register with your GSTR-1 summary.
  • Check the B2B section party by party for large customers.
  • Check the HSN summary: every line should have a code, and the totals should match.
  • Check that CGST/SGST and IGST are split correctly between local and interstate sales.

Before GSTR-3B: reconcile your input credit

Your eligible input tax credit is generally based on what your suppliers reported, which appears in GSTR-2B, a statement generated for you each month. Compare it with your purchase register:

SituationWhat to do
In your books and in 2BClaim the credit
In your books, missing from 2BAsk the supplier to file or correct; claim when it appears
In 2B, not in your booksFind the bill; it may be a missed entry, or not your purchase
Amounts differCheck for typing errors on either side

File GSTR-3B and pay

  1. Enter outward tax from your sales summary.
  2. Enter eligible input credit after reconciliation.
  3. Set off credit in the required order: IGST credit first, and CGST and SGST credit only against their own heads after that.
  4. Pay any balance in cash, and file before the due date to avoid interest and late fees.

Keep a filing calendar

Put the due dates in your phone with a reminder three days before. Late filing doesn't only cost you fees; it also delays your buyers' credit, and that hurts business relationships.
